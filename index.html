01 — The person

Who Was Anne Frank?

Anne Frank was a Jewish girl born in Frankfurt, Germany, on June 12, 1929. She is known for the diary she wrote during World War II, in which she described her thoughts and experiences while living in hiding.

In 1933, her family moved to the Netherlands to escape the Nazi regime. As persecution of Jewish people increased, Anne, her family, and four other people went into hiding in a secret annex in Amsterdam.

An Important Fact

Anne received her diary as a gift for her thirteenth birthday in 1942. She wrote about everyday life, her emotions, her dreams, and the events happening around her.

02 — Historical context

A Life During Wartime

World War II lasted from 1939 to 1945. Under the Nazi regime, millions of people were persecuted and murdered, especially Jewish people, in the genocide known as the Holocaust.

03 — Her testimony

Anne's Diary

The diary helps readers understand what life was like in hiding and what Anne thought during those years. It is not only a record of wartime events: it also explores growing up, living with others, having hopes, and discovering who you are.

I

Everyday Life

She describes the rules of hiding, living together, and the challenges of being unable to go outside freely.

II

Her Thoughts

She expresses emotions, disagreements, fears, ideas, and hopes typical of a teenager.

III

Writing

Writing became a way for Anne to express her experiences and preserve her memories.

Anne Frank's diary reminds us of the importance of listening to people's stories and preserving the memory of the past.

04 — The place

The Secret Annex

The hiding place was located at the back of the building where Otto Frank worked, at 263 Prinsengracht in Amsterdam. Its entrance was concealed behind a movable bookcase.

Eight people lived there for approximately two years. Some of Otto Frank's employees helped them by bringing food, news, and other necessary supplies.

Why Is It Important?

The site is preserved as part of the Anne Frank House, a museum that helps visitors understand the history of the people who hid there and the Nazi persecution.

05 — Her legacy

Why Does Her Story Still Matter?

Anne Frank's diary became one of the best-known testimonies of the Holocaust. Her story helps people remember the victims of Nazi persecution and reflect on discrimination, hatred, and human rights.

Reading her story encourages us to value freedom, respect differences, and recognize that behind historical events were real people with families, dreams, and hopes for the future.
